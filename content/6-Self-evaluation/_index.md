# Self-Assessment

During the **Workforce Bootcamp - First Cloud AI Journey (FCAJ)** program at **Amazon Web Services Viet Nam Company Limited**, I had the opportunity to explore a learning environment focused on cloud computing and apply the knowledge I had gained at university to a practical project.

Through the **Smart Kitchen (Bếp Thông Minh)** project, I gradually learned how to deploy an application on AWS rather than studying individual services in isolation. I practiced combining Amazon S3 and Amazon CloudFront to host the frontend; Amazon API Gateway and AWS Lambda to process application requests; Amazon DynamoDB to store recipe data; and Amazon Location Service to support nearby ingredient-store searches. I also explored Amazon Bedrock for AI-powered meal suggestions and used Amazon CloudWatch to review logs during testing.

In addition to project development, I spent time studying AWS documentation, recording implementation steps, and preparing workshop and internship report materials. This experience helped me understand that building a cloud application is not simply about making the system work. It also requires attention to access permissions, reliability, testing, cost, and the quality of technical documentation.

## What I Did Well

- **Learning through practice:** Rather than relying only on theory, I configured and tested how AWS services work together in a specific application.
- **Persistence in problem-solving:** When deployment issues occurred, I tried to inspect configurations, review logs, and consult documentation to find possible solutions.
- **Attention to documentation:** I recognized that recording implementation steps, test results, and important notes makes reviews and handovers easier.
- **Considering practical requirements:** While improving the project, I began paying more attention to service costs, access permissions, and service limitations instead of focusing only on features.

## Areas for Improvement

- **Developing architectural thinking:** I need to practice analyzing requirements and comparing alternative solutions before selecting services, especially in terms of security, reliability, and cost.
- **Improving troubleshooting skills:** I should establish a systematic investigation process, identify root causes, and document solutions to reduce excessive trial and error.
- **Managing time more effectively:** I should divide work into smaller tasks, set completion milestones, and prioritize issues that directly affect overall progress.
- **Improving presentation skills:** I need to practice explaining architecture, data flows, and technical decisions in a concise and understandable way.
- **Strengthening foundational knowledge:** I will continue learning about IAM, networking, application security, monitoring, and AWS cost optimization.

## Lessons Learned

The most important lesson I learned is that an application running successfully does not necessarily mean it is a complete cloud solution. Every technical decision can affect other aspects of the system, including access control, scalability, operations, and cost. Therefore, developers need to understand why a service is selected, know how to verify the results, and recognize any remaining limitations.

I also learned that encountering errors during hands-on practice is a normal part of learning. Instead of only trying to make an error disappear, I should spend time identifying its cause, reviewing my initial assumptions, and recording what I have learned for future use.

## Next Development Goals

1. Strengthen my AWS fundamentals and continue practicing the services used in the project.
2. Study the AWS Well-Architected Framework in greater depth, especially the Security, Reliability, Performance Efficiency, and Cost Optimization pillars.
3. Improve my ability to read logs, analyze errors, and test end-to-end application workflows.
4. Explore Infrastructure as Code and deployment automation to reduce manual configuration.
5. Maintain the habit of writing technical documentation and practice presenting solutions clearly with supporting evidence.

I consider FCAJ an experience that helped me move from learning cloud concepts individually to understanding how they can be applied to a practical product. The knowledge I gained and the limitations I identified during this process will serve as a foundation for further developing my professional skills in the future.
